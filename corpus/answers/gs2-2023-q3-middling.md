e-governance means the use of information technology for delivering government
services. It has improved effectiveness, transparency and accountability in many ways,
but there are also several inadequacies which reduce its benefits.

Among the achievements, Direct Benefit Transfer has reduced leakages in subsidy
delivery. Online portals have made many services available without visiting a
government office. The {{f:Right to Information Act, 2005|Right to Information Act,
2007}} has additionally been supported by online disclosure of information by
departments.

However there are many problems. The digital divide is a major issue because internet
penetration is low in many rural areas. Digital literacy is also limited, especially
among elderly people. Many citizens therefore depend on intermediaries, which defeats
the purpose of direct access.

There are also problems of infrastructure such as poor connectivity and frequent
server failures. Cyber security is another concern, and the
{{f:Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023|Digital Personal Data Protection Act,
2019}} has only recently addressed data protection. Grievance redressal mechanisms are
often weak, so citizens have no remedy when the system fails. Unless these gaps are
addressed, the benefits of e-governance will remain limited to a section of the
population.
