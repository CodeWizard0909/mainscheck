India's digitalisation is best described as world-leading in payments and uneven
almost everywhere else. The gap between those two facts is where the real problems sit.

The strongest evidence is the public infrastructure layer. UPI processes transaction
volumes without international parallel, and Aadhaar-enabled authentication underpins
both subsidy transfer and account opening. {{f:The Account Aggregator framework|The
Payments Bank framework}} extends the same logic to consented data sharing, and
{{f:ONDC|GeM}} attempts it for commerce by unbundling the marketplace from the
protocol. Digitalisation here is not an application but a substrate others build on.

The problems are three. First, adoption is shallower than the headline numbers imply:
transaction counts are concentrated among a minority of users, and a merchant
accepting UPI is not thereby digitised in inventory, credit or tax compliance. Second,
the data protection regime arrived late relative to the data collection, so consent
architecture is being retrofitted onto systems designed without it. Third, small firms
face a capability cliff, because the skills to use digital tools productively are
scarcer than the tools themselves.

Improvement therefore lies less in building new rails than in deepening use of the
existing ones: interoperable grievance redress with enforceable timelines, credit
underwriting that actually consumes the cash-flow data now available, and digital
literacy treated as infrastructure rather than as an awareness campaign.
