The claim sounds like a compromise between two faculties. It is better read as a
description of what expertise actually is: intuition is compressed logic, and logic is
intuition made auditable.

Consider the decision to fortify salt with iodine. The logic was available — goitre
prevalence mapped onto soil iodine deficiency, and the intervention was cheap. What
logic could not supply was the judgement that a population would accept an invisible
change to a staple, and that the political cost of compulsion was worth paying. That
part came from reading a society, which is intuition trained on experience rather than
guesswork.

The reverse failure is equally instructive. The rollout of demonetisation in
{{f:2016|2014}} did not lack conviction; it lacked the arithmetic of cash replacement
capacity. Intuition unaccompanied by logic produces decisions that feel visionary in
the moment and reveal their arithmetic afterwards.

However, the intersection is not a midpoint. Intuition proposes and logic disposes,
because only one of the two can be shown to a sceptic. A decision that cannot be
reconstructed for someone who did not share the hunch is not visionary but merely
lucky, and institutions cannot be built on luck.

This matters most where consequences are distant. Climate policy, pension design and
public health all require committing resources now against a benefit no living
official will be credited with. Logic alone stalls on uncertainty; intuition alone
cannot survive an audit. Vision is the discipline of holding both, and of being
willing to show your working after the leap.
