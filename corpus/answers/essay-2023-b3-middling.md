Justice and charity are two different concepts. Justice means giving every person
their rightful due. Charity means helping the poor and needy voluntarily. The statement
says that if there is more justice then less charity will be required.

This is true in many ways. If wages are fair then workers will not need donations. If
education is available to all then poor students will not need scholarships from
individuals. Justice removes the root cause of the problem while charity only provides
temporary relief.

India has moved in this direction through rights based legislation. The
{{f:MGNREGA|PMGSY}} was enacted in {{f:2005|2001}} and it gave a legal guarantee of
employment. The Right to Education Act made education a fundamental right for children.
The National Food Security Act made subsidised food a legal entitlement rather than a
favour.

However, charity also has an important role. Government schemes take time to reach
everyone. Natural disasters require immediate help. Many NGOs work in areas where the
State is not present. Therefore charity cannot be completely replaced.

In conclusion, a just society should aim to reduce the need for charity by ensuring
rights and opportunities for all. But charity will continue to be needed for immediate
relief and for filling the gaps in government systems.
