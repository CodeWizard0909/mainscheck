The share of manufacturing in India's GDP has remained around
{{f:16-17 per cent|26-27 per cent}} for a long time. To achieve faster economic growth
this share needs to increase, and MSMEs are very important because they provide
employment to a large number of people.

The government has taken several policy measures. The Production Linked Incentive
scheme gives incentives based on additional production in selected sectors. The
{{f:Make in India|Startup India}} initiative was launched to promote manufacturing.
Credit guarantee schemes have been introduced to help MSMEs get loans without
collateral.

The definition of MSMEs was also revised in {{f:2020|2015}} to include turnover along
with investment. This was done because many firms were avoiding expansion in order to
retain their MSME benefits. Schemes like Udyam registration have additionally
simplified the process of registration.

However there are still many problems. Delayed payments from buyers affect the working
capital of small firms. Compliance requirements are complex and costly. Infrastructure
and logistics costs are high compared to competing countries. Therefore the government
should focus on easing these constraints along with providing incentives, so that
MSMEs can grow and contribute more to manufacturing.
