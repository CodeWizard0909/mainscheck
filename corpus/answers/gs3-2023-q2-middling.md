Digitalisation of the Indian economy has progressed very fast in the last decade. The
most important example is the Unified Payments Interface which was launched in
{{f:2016|2012}} by NPCI. It has made digital payments easy for crores of people.

Other examples are also there. Aadhaar is used for authentication and for direct
benefit transfer. {{f:GST|VAT}} has created a common digital platform for indirect
taxes. Government e-Marketplace is used for public procurement. Many services are
available online because of the Digital India programme.

However several problems are faced in this regard. The digital divide is the biggest
problem because internet access is not uniform across regions. Digital literacy is
also low, especially among older people and in rural areas. Cyber frauds have
increased along with the growth of digital payments.

Small businesses also face difficulties in adopting digital tools because of cost and
lack of skills. Data privacy was another concern, which has been addressed by the
{{f:Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023|Digital Personal Data Protection Act,
2018}}. To improve the situation the government should expand broadband connectivity,
conduct digital literacy programmes and strengthen cyber security systems.
