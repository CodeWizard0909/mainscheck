The use of Article 356 has come down since the mid-1990s because of both legal and
political reasons. Earlier it was used very frequently, and many State governments were
dismissed by the Union government for political purposes.

The main legal reason is the judgment in {{f:S. R. Bommai v. Union of India
(1994)|S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1999)}}. The Supreme Court said that the
proclamation is subject to judicial review and that the majority of a government should
be tested on the floor of the House. This reduced the scope for the Governor to report
that the government had lost majority without an actual test.

The political reasons are also important. After {{f:1989|1979}} coalition governments
became common at the Centre and regional parties became stronger. These parties were
part of the ruling coalition and therefore opposed the misuse of
{{f:Article 356|Article 365}} because it could be used against them later.

The Sarkaria Commission had also recommended that this power should be used only in
rare cases. Because of all these factors the frequency of President's Rule has reduced
considerably in the last three decades, although it has not stopped completely.
