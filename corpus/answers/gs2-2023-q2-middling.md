The Constitution provides for gender justice through several provisions in the
Fundamental Rights and the Directive Principles. The basic idea is that women should
get equality not only in law but also in actual practice.

{{f:Article 14|Article 16}} provides for equality before law and equal protection of
laws. Article 15(1) prohibits discrimination on the ground of sex. Article 15(3) is
also important because it allows the State to make special provisions for women and
children. Article 16 provides equality of opportunity in public employment.

The Directive Principles additionally support gender justice.
{{f:Article 39|Article 41}} directs the State to provide equal means of livelihood and
equal pay for equal work. Article 42 deals with maternity relief and humane conditions
of work.

Many case laws have also helped. In {{f:Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan|Vishaka v. State
of Gujarat}} the Supreme Court gave guidelines against sexual harassment at the
workplace. In the Babita Puniya case the Court allowed permanent commission for women
officers in the Army. These judgments show that the judiciary has played an important
role in expanding gender justice, even though problems remain in the area of personal
laws.
